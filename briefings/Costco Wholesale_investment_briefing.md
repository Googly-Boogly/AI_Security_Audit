# Costco Wholesale Corporation — Investment Briefing

**Ticker:** `COST` (Nasdaq) · **Sector:** Consumer Staples — broadline retail / membership warehouse clubs · **AS OF: 2026-09-07**
**RECOMMENDATION: HOLD — Conviction: Medium**

## The Call

Hold an existing position through the five-year horizon; do not commit new capital until the entry multiple is established. The single reason to own it: membership fees are ~2% of total revenue but historically approximate a majority of operating income [HIGH] — a near-100%-margin annuity that passes the quality test on its own. The reason not to add: the dossier's ~$400bn market cap is tagged [LOW] and explicitly not current, so no multiple can be quoted.

## The Business

Costco runs paid-membership warehouse clubs: members buy a deliberately narrow assortment in bulk at low markup in plain warehouses. Each warehouse carries roughly 3,500–4,000 items against tens of thousands at a supermarket, which concentrates buying power and turns inventory fast [HIGH]. Costco caps its own markup at about 14% on branded goods and 15% on Kirkland Signature private label, so merchandise earns very little and the annual fee carries the profit [HIGH]. Ancillary operations — gasoline, pharmacy, optical, food court, travel — sit alongside the warehouses; gasoline is large, volatile and low-margin enough to distort reported comparable sales [HIGH].

## Financial Snapshot

- FY2024: total revenue ~$254bn (net sales ~$250bn + membership fees ~$4.8bn); net income ~$7.4bn [MED]
- FY2024: ~77m paid member households / ~140m cardholders; renewal ~92–93% US/Canada, ~90% worldwide [MED]
- FY2024 and prior several years: operating margin ~3.5% of total revenue, drifted marginally higher rather than stepped; management does not manage for margin expansion [HIGH]
- FY2024: operating cash flow ~$11–12bn against capex ~$4.7bn; net cash (long-term debt ~$5.8bn vs cash and short-term investments ~$11bn+) [MED]
- Through FY2024: mid-single-digit revenue growth from low-to-mid-single-digit comps ex-gas and FX plus ~25–30 new warehouses a year on an ~890 base [HIGH cadence / MED base]
- FY2025 (ended 31 Aug 2025): full-year figures not held with confidence; ~$270–280bn revenue is order-of-magnitude only, treat as a lead [LOW]

## Why It Could Work

- Fee annuity with demonstrated pricing power: the September 2024 move to $65/$130 was the first increase since 2017 [MED], dropping through at near-100% margin onto a majority-of-operating-income base [HIGH].
- Self-funded growth: ~3% annual unit growth sits well inside operating cash flow on a net cash balance sheet [MED], with Other International at ~14% of sales as optionality, not thesis [MED].
- A moat that widens with scale: renewal rates have trended up, not down, and no competitor has replicated the fee-funded model at Costco's scale [MED]; scale gains are spent widening the price gap [HIGH].

## What Could Break It

- Multiple compression with nothing operationally wrong: EPS growth is capped near revenue growth plus a ~$0.7bn/yr buyback [MED], so a 25–30% de-rating over five years would erase the whole earnings compound.
- A renewal slip is unhedged: with markup capped by stated policy [HIGH], a 2–3 point renewal decline would flow almost entirely into operating income, and post-increase renewal rates are unverified.
- Recent growth may be flattered by items that lap: the deferred fee increase [MED], tightened card-sharing enforcement [MED] and low-margin bullion sales [LOW].

## What Would Change Our Mind

- US/Canada renewal rate prints below 90% (from ~92–93% [MED]) in any 10-K or quarterly disclosure — the annuity, not the retailer, is the asset.
- Membership fee income growth turns negative ex-FX once the September 2024 increase fully laps, or the annual opening plan is guided below ~20 warehouses.
- Operating margin expansion becomes a stated management objective under the new CFO [MED], or operating margin falls below ~3.0% on unabsorbed wage or tariff cost.

The load-bearing business facts carry HIGH tags; the price does not. **Overall confidence: Medium.**
