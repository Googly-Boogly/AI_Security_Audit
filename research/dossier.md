## Business Model

- Membership warehouse club: paid members buy a deliberately narrow assortment in bulk at low markup in no-frills warehouses. [HIGH]
- SKU count deliberately restricted to roughly 3,500–4,000 per warehouse versus tens of thousands at a supermarket; drives purchasing leverage and inventory turns. [HIGH]
- Self-imposed markup ceiling: ~14% on branded goods, ~15% on Kirkland Signature private label. [HIGH]
- Kirkland Signature private label is roughly one-third of total sales. [MED]
- Membership tiers: Gold Star and Executive; Executive earns a 2% annual reward on qualifying purchases. US Gold Star $65 / Executive $130 following the September 2024 increase (prior $60/$120; first increase since 2017). [MED]
- Where the money actually comes from: net sales carry a very thin margin; membership fees are ~2% of total revenue but historically approximate a majority of operating income. This is the core structural insight. [HIGH]
- Membership fee revenue: FY2023 ~$4.6bn; FY2024 ~$4.8bn. [MED]
- Paid members ~77m households, ~140m cardholders (FY2024). Renewal rate ~92–93% US/Canada, ~90% worldwide. [MED]
- Geographic mix of net sales, FY2024: US ~72–73%, Canada ~13–14%, Other International ~14%. Reported as three segments: United States, Canada, Other International. [MED]
- Ancillary and other businesses: gasoline stations, pharmacy, optical, hearing aids, food court, tire installation, Costco Travel, and a Citi-issued co-branded Visa card. Gasoline is a large, volatile, low-margin revenue line that distorts reported comps. [HIGH]
- E-commerce is a small single-digit share of sales; Costco Next is a curated third-party drop-ship marketplace. [MED]
- Labor posture: pays above retail-sector median wages; low turnover is treated internally as a cost advantage rather than a cost. Top-of-scale hourly ~$30 as recalled from 2024. [HIGH] / figure [LOW]

## Financial Profile

- FY2023 total revenue ~$242bn (net sales ~$238bn + membership fees ~$4.6bn); net income ~$6.3bn. [MED]
- FY2024 total revenue ~$254bn (net sales ~$250bn + membership fees ~$4.8bn); net income ~$7.4bn. [MED]
- FY2025 (ended August 31, 2025) full-year figures: I do not have these with confidence. Order of magnitude only, ~$270–280bn revenue. Treat as a lead. [LOW]
- Growth trajectory: mid-single-digit total revenue growth, driven by low-to-mid-single-digit comparable sales excluding gas and FX plus ~25–30 new warehouses per year. [HIGH]
- Gross margin: structurally low, roughly 10–11% of net sales; direction has been slightly upward as gasoline deflation and mix shifts flatter the ratio. [HIGH] / recent direction [MED]
- Operating margin: roughly 3.5% of total revenue; has drifted marginally higher over the last several fiscal years, not stepped. Costco does not manage for margin expansion — it recycles scale gains into price. [HIGH]
- Net margin: roughly 2.5–3% (FY2024). [MED]
- Cash generation: strongly cash-generative; operating cash flow order of magnitude ~$11–12bn in FY2024, capex ~$4.7bn concentrated in new warehouses and depots. [MED]
- Balance sheet: net cash. Long-term debt ~$5.8bn (FY2024) against cash and short-term investments of roughly $11bn+. Leverage is not an analytical issue here. [MED]
- Capital returns: modest regular dividend (raised to $1.16/quarter in April 2024), small buybacks (~$0.7bn/yr), and periodic large special dividends — $15/share paid January 2024 (~$6.7bn), following $10 in 2020, $7 in 2017. [MED]
- Inventory model generates negative working capital: payables fund inventory given rapid turns. [HIGH]

## Competitive Position

- Named principal competitors: Walmart (including Sam's Club), BJ's Wholesale Club, Target, Kroger, Amazon, Aldi and Lidl, Trader Joe's; in Canada, Loblaw and Metro. [HIGH]
- Edge: scale purchasing on a narrow SKU base, membership fee annuity that decouples profit from merchandise margin, sales per square foot well above peers, brand trust in Kirkland Signature, and low labor turnover. [HIGH]
- The edge is structural rather than technological and has been widening slowly, not eroding — renewal rates have trended up, not down, and no competitor has replicated the fee-funded model at Costco's scale. [MED]
- Genuine pressures: Sam's Club price and digital investment, Amazon in non-food general merchandise, Aldi/Lidl on grocery price perception, and e-commerce delivery economics where Costco is a follower. [MED]
- International (particularly China and Japan) is the growth optionality; unit economics on new Asian openings have been reported as above-system-average. [LOW]

## Recent Developments

- CEO transition: Ron Vachris became CEO effective January 1, 2024, succeeding Craig Jelinek. [HIGH]
- CFO transition: Gary Millerchip became CFO in March 2024, succeeding Richard Galanti after ~40 years. This is the most material change to disclosure style and guidance posture in decades. [MED]
- Membership fee increase announced July 2024, effective September 1, 2024 — flows into reported fee income on a deferred basis across FY2025–FY2026. [MED]
- Enforcement of membership card sharing tightened via photo-ID scanners at entrances (2023–2024). [MED]
- January 2025 annual meeting: shareholders rejected an anti-DEI proposal by a very wide margin (~98%). [MED]
- US tariff policy changes in 2025 prompted commentary on re-sourcing and pull-forward of imports; quantified P&L impact not known to me. [LOW]
- Gold and silver bullion sales became a non-trivial, low-margin comp contributor (2024–2025). [LOW]
- No material M&A; Costco grows organically. [HIGH]
- Company reports monthly sales, which means the sell-side narrative moves on data I do not have. [HIGH]
