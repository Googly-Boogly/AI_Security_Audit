# Costco Wholesale (`COST`) — Investment Case

*Mandate: long-only quality compounder fund, 5-year horizon. Source: `research/dossier.md` (AS OF 2026-09-07, compiled without live data). Confidence tags carried through.*

## Bear Case

1. **You lose money without anything going wrong operationally — pure multiple compression.** Management explicitly does not manage for margin expansion and recycles scale gains into price; operating margin has "drifted marginally higher, not stepped," and sits near 3.5% of total revenue [HIGH]. So EPS growth is capped near revenue growth (mid-single-digit [HIGH]) plus a ~$0.7bn/yr buyback [MED]. Against a recalled ~$400bn market cap [LOW] and FY2024 net income of ~$7.4bn [MED] — roughly 54x — a 25–30% de-rating over five years erases the entire earnings compound. This is the highest-probability loss path and it requires no bad news.
2. **The profit engine is a fee annuity, and a renewal-rate slip is levered ~30x.** Membership fees are ~2% of revenue but historically approximate a majority of operating income [HIGH]; FY2024 fee revenue ~$4.8bn [MED] on ~77m paid households with ~92–93% US/Canada renewal [MED]. Because the merchandise business is capped by a self-imposed 14%/15% markup ceiling [HIGH], there is no offsetting lever if renewals fall — a 2–3 point renewal decline flows almost entirely to operating income. Severity: severe; and the dossier's own open question flags that post-increase renewal rates are unverified.
3. **FY2025–26 growth is flattered by one-time items that lap.** The September 2024 fee increase (first since 2017) flows into fee income on a deferred basis across FY2025–FY2026 [MED], card-sharing enforcement was tightened via ID scanners in 2023–24 [MED], and gold/silver bullion became a non-trivial low-margin comp contributor [LOW] on top of gasoline, which already distorts reported comps [HIGH]. If the market has extrapolated a step-change as a trend, the lap produces a growth disappointment against a premium multiple. Severity: moderate-to-high given multiple sensitivity.
4. **Cost inflation that policy forbids passing through compresses a 3.5% margin.** Costco pays above retail-median wages by design, with a Teamsters contract renegotiated around 2024 on unverified terms, and 2025 tariff changes prompted re-sourcing commentary with unquantified P&L impact [LOW]. On ~3.5% operating margin [HIGH], roughly 35bp of unabsorbed cost is ~10% of operating income. Severity: moderate, but the sensitivity is asymmetric because the markup ceiling is a stated commitment, not a dial.

## Bull Case

1. **A subscription annuity with ~7-year pricing power attached.** Fees are near-100%-margin drop-through and a majority of operating income [HIGH]; the September 2024 move to $65/$130 was the first increase since 2017 [MED] across ~77m households [MED], and Executive tier penetration is the lever management is actively pushing (unverified magnitude). Magnitude: a mid-single-digit percentage uplift to fee income compounds into a high-single-digit-plus uplift to operating income.
2. **Self-funded unit growth with a long runway.** ~25–30 new warehouses per year on an ~890 base [HIGH growth cadence / MED base] is ~3% unit growth, additive to low-to-mid-single-digit comps ex-gas and FX [HIGH]. Capex of ~$4.7bn sits well inside ~$11–12bn operating cash flow [MED] against a net cash balance sheet [MED], so growth needs no external capital. Other International is only ~14% of sales [MED] with Asian unit economics reported above system average [LOW] — optionality, not thesis.
3. **A moat that widens with scale rather than requiring reinvention.** 3,500–4,000 SKUs versus tens of thousands at a supermarket drives purchasing leverage and turns [HIGH]; Kirkland is ~one-third of sales [MED]; renewal rates have trended up, not down, and no competitor has replicated the fee-funded model at scale [MED]. The markup ceiling means scale gains are spent widening the price gap — the mechanism that makes trade-down environments a share gain rather than a margin event.
4. **Cash generation exceeds reported earnings in economic terms.** Negative working capital means payables fund inventory [HIGH], and the special-dividend cadence — $15/share January 2024 (~$6.7bn), $10 in 2020, $7 in 2017 [MED] — returns balance-sheet accumulation that never appears in the EPS line. Magnitude: episodic 1.5–2% distributions on top of a modest regular dividend.

## Valuation Frame

This is an earnings-multiple business with a sum-of-parts overlay: the fee stream is a subscription annuity deserving a subscription multiple, and the merchandise operation is a thin-margin retailer deserving a retail multiple. Value accrues to whoever capitalises the annuity correctly.

**The dossier gives no reliable current price or multiple.** Market cap is tagged [LOW] and explicitly "do not treat as current"; FY2025 revenue is an order-of-magnitude lead [LOW] and FY2025 net income is absent. I will not quote a multiple.

So: **what must be true at the current price?** With margin expansion disavowed as policy [HIGH], EPS grows at roughly revenue growth plus ~0.2% from buybacks [MED] — call it 7–9% annually. A 5-year return therefore equals that compound minus any de-rating. To clear a 10% hurdle, the entry multiple must hold *and* the fee lever must repeat. If the multiple is anywhere near the ~54x implied by the [LOW] $400bn recollection against [MED] FY2024 earnings, the buyer is paying for flawless execution and receiving no compensation for the bear cases above.

## Recommendation

RECOMMENDATION: HOLD — Conviction: Medium

The quality test for a compounder mandate is passed on HIGH-tagged evidence — fee-funded economics, structural cost advantage, self-funded unit growth, net cash — so an existing position should be held through the five-year horizon rather than trimmed on price alone. New capital is a different question: the entry multiple is the dominant determinant of the 5-year outcome and the dossier cannot support one, so I decline to underwrite a purchase until the three items below are checked. This is not INSUFFICIENT DATA — the load-bearing *business* facts carry HIGH tags; only the price does not, and price is verifiable rather than unknowable.

## Kill Criteria

- **US/Canada renewal rate prints below 90%** (from ~92–93% [MED]) in any 10-K or quarterly disclosure — the annuity, not the retailer, is the asset.
- **Membership fee income growth turns negative ex-FX once the September 2024 increase fully laps**, or the annual opening plan is guided below ~20 warehouses — both growth levers exhausted simultaneously.
- **Operating margin expansion becomes a stated management objective** under the new CFO [MED], or operating margin falls below ~3.0% on unabsorbed wage/tariff cost — the first means the price gap is being harvested rather than widened; the second means it cannot be defended.

## Verify Before Acting

- **Current share price, share count and FY2025 net income** (FY2025 10-K, filed ~October 2025). Replaces the [LOW] ~$400bn market cap and [LOW] ~$270–280bn revenue lead. The entire recommendation hinges on this; nothing else is close.
- **FY2025/FY2026 membership fee income, renewal rates post-increase, and Executive penetration as a share of members and of sales.** Confirms whether the [MED] fee-increase flow-through arrived without a renewal cost.
- **Segment operating income by geography, FY2026 opening plan and capex guidance, plus measured tariff and gasoline effects on gross margin from transcripts.** Tests the unit-growth runway and the [LOW] cost-absorption exposure with disclosed numbers rather than inference.
